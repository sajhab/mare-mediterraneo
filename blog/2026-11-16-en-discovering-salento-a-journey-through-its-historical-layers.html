The Ancient Greeks and Magna Graecia in Salento

Salento's relationship with the Greeks dates back centuries, when this beautiful region was part of Magna Graecia. Greek settlers arrived around the 8th century BC, bringing their rich culture and laying the foundations for Salento's historical tapestry. Visitors can explore the archaeological site at Roca Vecchia, just an hour's drive from Mare Mediterraneo, where ancient Greek relics whisper tales of the past.

Romans: Engineering Wonders and Roads That Connect

Following the Greeks, the Romans left an indelible mark on Salento. Their engineering prowess is evident in the roads that connect this region to the wider Roman world. One such road is the ancient Via Traiana, which reached Brindisi, paving the way for commerce and culture. Travelers can visit the splendid Roman amphitheater in Lecce, showcasing the grandeur of Roman architecture and only a short drive from Torre Chianca.

The Normans and Their Medieval Legacy

In the 11th century, the Normans brought new life to Salento, establishing fortresses and castles. Their legacy can be explored at the Castello di Copertino, 20 minutes from Mare Mediterraneo, where medieval walls speak of battles won and lost. This era marks a cultural renaissance, intertwining Norman influences with local traditions, visible in the region's architecture.

The Era of Baroque: A Symphony of Artistic Flourish

The Baroque period swept through Lecce in the 17th century, transforming the city into a showcase of grandiose architecture. Known as the Florence of the South, Lecce's stone facades and intricate details must be experienced firsthand. The Basilica di Santa Croce is a highlight, a mere 15 minutes from Torre Chianca, where visitors can marvel at the artistry.
